# Kidney Disease Clinical Practice Guidelines: Serum Creatinine and Renal Monitoring

**Source Organization:** National Kidney Foundation / KDIGO Practice Guidelines  
**Publication Date:** 2025-10-15  
**Document ID:** DOC-KDIGO-CREATININE-2025  
**Category:** Laboratory & Renal Safety Guideline  

## Serum Creatinine and Glomerular Filtration Rate Assessment
Serum creatinine is the primary endogenous filtration marker used to estimate glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Because serum creatinine alone is influenced by muscle mass, age, sex, and hydration, clinical guidelines mandate calculating eGFR using validated equations (such as CKD-EPI) rather than relying solely on raw creatinine concentrations.

## Normal Reference Ranges and Clinical Interpretation
- Typical reference range for adults is approximately 0.6 to 1.2 mg/dL (depending on sex, age, and laboratory methodology).
- A stable serum creatinine level of 1.0 mg/dL generally corresponds to preserved baseline renal function in average-weight adults (eGFR >= 60 mL/min/1.73m2).
- An increase of >= 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours, or an increase >= 1.5 times baseline within 7 days, defines acute kidney injury (AKI) requiring urgent evaluation.

## Monitoring Protocol for Medication Safety
Patients taking renally cleared medications (such as metformin, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, SGLT2 inhibitors, or diuretics) require baseline renal function testing:
- **Annual Monitoring:** In stable patients with normal baseline renal function (eGFR >= 60 mL/min/1.73m2).
- **Semiannual or Quarterly Monitoring:** In patients with mild-to-moderate impairment (eGFR 30–59 mL/min/1.73m2), older adults, or following medication dosage adjustments.
- Serum creatinine and potassium must be re-checked within 2 to 4 weeks following dose increases in renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) inhibitors.
