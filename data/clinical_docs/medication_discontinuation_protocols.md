# Clinical Guidance on Medication Discontinuation, Deprescribing, and Patient-Initiated Cessation

**Source Organization:** Clinical Pharmacotherapy Deprescribing Network  
**Publication Date:** 2026-01-05  
**Document ID:** DOC-DEPRESCRIBING-SAFETY-2026  
**Category:** Clinical Safety & Medication Protocol  

## Principles of Safe Medication Discontinuation
Medication discontinuation (deprescribing) is the planned and supervised process of dose reduction or stopping of drugs that may causing harm or no longer be providing benefit. When evaluating medications that were discontinued or stopped:
- Document the precise rationale for discontinuation (adverse effect, lack of therapeutic indication, patient intolerance, drug-drug interaction).
- Record the timeline and cessation date.
- Confirm whether the cessation was provider-directed or patient self-initiated.

## Patient-Initiated Medication Cessation
When a patient reports having stopped a medication on their own (e.g., "Patient says they stopped Drug B approximately one week ago"):
1. Verify whether the prescribing record in the electronic medication database matches the cessation.
2. Inquire about symptoms or side effects that prompted the patient to stop the medication.
3. Assess for rebound phenomena, withdrawal symptoms, or recurrence of the underlying medical condition.
4. Update the active medication list to prevent inadvertent refills or duplicate therapy.

## Reconciling Active vs Discontinued Status
Discrepancies where one system records a medication as discontinued while past clinical notes record it as active represent expected chronological transitions. When recent clinical consultations and updated medication databases concur that a drug has ceased, the clinical record should reflect the discontinued status, retaining the historical context for clinical provenance.
