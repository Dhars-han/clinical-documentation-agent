# ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes: Metformin Therapy and Renal Safety

**Source Organization:** American Diabetes Association (ADA)  
**Publication Date:** 2026-01-15  
**Document ID:** DOC-ADA-METFORMIN-2026  
**Category:** Clinical Practice Guideline  

## Overview and First-Line Indications
Metformin remains the preferred initial pharmacologic agent for the treatment of type 2 diabetes in adults, in conjunction with lifestyle modifications (healthy eating, weight management, and physical activity). Metformin is effective, safe, inexpensive, and associated with weight neutrality or modest weight reduction and low risk of hypoglycemia when used as monotherapy.

## Recommended Dosing and Titration
The usual starting dose of metformin is 500 mg orally once daily with a meal (typically the evening meal) to minimize gastrointestinal side effects. If well tolerated, the dose can be titrated by 500 mg every 1 to 2 weeks to a therapeutic target dose of 1,000 mg twice daily or 2,000 mg once daily (for extended-release formulations). The maximum effective daily dose is typically 2,000 to 2,550 mg daily.

## Renal Function Monitoring and Contraindications
Renal function must be assessed prior to initiating metformin and at least annually thereafter. Patients at risk for renal impairment or with borderline renal function require more frequent monitoring (every 3 to 6 months).
- **eGFR >= 60 mL/min/1.73m2:** No dose adjustment necessary; annual renal function monitoring.
- **eGFR 45 to 59 mL/min/1.73m2:** Metformin may be continued; monitor renal function every 3 to 6 months.
- **eGFR 30 to 44 mL/min/1.73m2:** Initiation of metformin is not recommended. If already taking metformin, evaluate risk-benefit and consider dose reduction (e.g., maximum 1,000 mg daily).
- **eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2:** Metformin is contraindicated due to increased risk of lactic acidosis.

## Gastrointestinal Tolerance and Vitamin B12 Monitoring
Common side effects of metformin include gastrointestinal disturbances (diarrhea, nausea, abdominal cramping). Extended-release metformin may improve gastrointestinal tolerance. Long-term use of metformin is associated with biochemical vitamin B12 deficiency; periodic measurement of vitamin B12 levels should be considered, particularly in patients with anemia or peripheral neuropathy.
